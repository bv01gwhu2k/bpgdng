How Much ALA Should You Take for Diabetic Nerve Health?

Diabetic peripheral neuropathy—nerve damage caused by chronically high blood sugar—can lead to tingling, numbness or pain in the hands and feet. Research shows that alpha-lipoic acid (ALA), a powerful antioxidant, may help protect nerve cells, ease symptoms and improve nerve conduction. Below, we explore the science behind ALA, the optimal Alpha lipoic acid dosage for nerves, safety considerations and practical tips for anyone considering this supplement.

What Is Alpha-Lipoic Acid?

Alpha-lipoic acid is a vitamin-like compound that:

Occurs naturally in every cell, helping convert glucose into energy

Acts as both water- and fat-soluble antioxidant, neutralizing free radicals

Regenerates other antioxidants (vitamin C, vitamin E, glutathione)

Because it crosses the blood-brain barrier and circulates throughout the body, ALA may offer unique protection to nerve fibers exposed to oxidative stress in diabetes.

How ALA Supports Nerve Health

Clinical studies suggest ALA can:

Improve nerve conduction velocity (how fast nerves send signals)

Reduce burning, tingling and numbness

Enhance microcirculation (blood flow to small nerve vessels)

Lower markers of oxidative stress and inflammation

By combating free-radical damage and boosting blood flow, ALA addresses two key drivers of diabetic neuropathy.

Clinical Evidence & Dosages Studied

Multiple randomized, placebo-controlled trials have evaluated ALA for diabetic neuropathy:

600 mg oral ALA once daily for 4–6 weeks• Result: significant reduction in pain and sensory symptoms

800 mg/day oral ALA for 5 weeks• Result: improved nerve conduction and vibration perception

Intravenous ALA (600 mg IV daily) for 3 weeks• Result: rapid symptom relief, but requires medical setting

A 2012 meta-analysis (ALA in Diabetic Neuropathy Study Group) concluded that 600 mg/day oral ALA for at least 3 weeks consistently improved neuropathic symptoms.

Recommended Alpha Lipoic Acid Dosage for Nerves

Based on current evidence, these general guidelines apply:

Starting dose: 300 mg once daily, taken on an empty stomach (30 minutes before meals)

Effective dose: 600 mg daily (either as a single 600 mg dose or 300 mg twice daily)

Maximum dose: up to 1,200 mg daily in divided doses (under physician supervision)

Key points:

Most studies use 600 mg/day for 4–12 weeks. Longer use beyond 6 months should be monitored by a healthcare professional.

For more severe symptoms, some clinicians may increase to 800–1,000 mg/day, but this should only occur under medical guidance.

Choosing the Right ALA Supplement

When shopping for ALA:

Look for pure, pharmaceutical-grade ALA standardized to 99% purity

Choose stabilized or "R-ALA" formulations (the biologically active form)

Avoid cheap, waxy-coated tablets that may not dissolve properly

Check for third-party testing (e.g., USP, NSF) on the label

Taking ALA on an empty stomach enhances absorption. If you experience mild stomach upset, you can split the dose (e.g., 300 mg morning, 300 mg evening) and take with a light snack.

Gastrointestinal upset (nausea, diarrhea)

Integrating ALA into a Comprehensive Plan

Alpha-lipoic acid should complement—not replace—standard diabetic care:

Maintain blood sugar within target range

Follow a balanced diet rich in whole grains, healthy fats, lean protein and colorful vegetables

Engage in regular physical activity (walking, swimming, resistance training)

Manage blood pressure and cholesterol

Avoid smoking and limit alcohol

Such lifestyle strategies work synergistically with ALA to protect nerves and optimize overall health.
