What is Male Hypogonadism?

Male hypogonadism is a condition in which the body produces insufficient testosterone, either due to problems in the testicles themselves or in the signaling system that controls testosterone production. This hormone deficiency disrupts the balance necessary for strength, energy, mood, sexual function, and more.

There are two main types of hypogonadism: primary hypogonadism and secondary hypogonadism.

Primary hypogonadism starts in the testicles. The testes can’t produce enough testosterone, even if the brain is sending the right signals. Causes include injury, infection, or genetic conditions like Klinefelter syndrome, which results from an abnormality of the sex chromosomes (X and Y).(1)

Symptoms of Male Hypogonadism

Hypogonadism in males may present itself with various physical, emotional, and cognitive symptoms. For many men, these changes come on gradually, which is partially why they’re frequently dismissed as part of the normal aging process.

Common signs of testosterone deficiency include fatigue and low energy, low libido, erectile dysfunction, loss of muscle mass or strength, increased body fat (particularly around the abdomen), depression or mood swings, and difficulty concentrating or mental fog.

If you’re noticing one or more of these symptoms, it may be time to consider a medical evaluation. Left untreated, these symptoms can worsen and begin to impact relationships, performance, and daily functioning.

Causes and Risk Factors

Several factors can contribute to the development of male hypogonadism. While some are within your control, others are related to age or underlying medical issues. The most common causes and risk factors of male hypogonadism include:

Aging and natural testosterone decline: Testosterone levels typically begin to decline by about 1% per year around the ages of 30 to 40. (6) A testosterone deficiency that develops later in life is referred to as late-onset hypogonadism, and may lead to other symptoms associated with low T.

Testicular injury or disease: Trauma, infection (such as mumps orchitis), or radiation/chemotherapy can damage the testes, which can lead to a decrease in testosterone. (7)

Pituitary or hypothalamic disorders: Tumors or genetic issues may disrupt the brain’s ability to send proper hormonal signals, which can lead to a lowering of your testosterone levels.

Chronic illness: Conditions like diabetes, obesity, and metabolic syndrome are closely linked to reduced testosterone levels. For example, one study showed that nearly 97% of diagnosed metabolic syndrome patients experienced issues with erectile dysfunction. (8)

Medications or anabolic steroid use: Certain drugs, including opioids and corticosteroids, have been shown to potentially interfere with hormone production, which can lead to male hypogonadism.

Lifestyle factors: Certain poor lifestyle habits, such as poor sleep, alcohol overuse, and high stress may also contribute by disrupting hormonal balance over time.

How is Male Hypogonadism Diagnosed?

If you suspect that you’re suffering from low testosterone, the first step is getting properly evaluated by a trusted medical provider, such as the clinicians at your local Gameday Men’s Health. Diagnosis involves a combination of blood work, symptom analysis, and clinical history.

Key steps in the diagnostic process include:

Blood tests: A lab panel—typically taken in the morning—measures total and free testosterone levels. Additional hormones such as LH (luteinizing hormone) and FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) may also be evaluated to determine whether the cause is primary or secondary. (9)

Medical history and symptom review: Your provider will ask about energy levels, sexual function, sleep, emotional well-being, and physical changes.

Additional tests: In some cases, imaging studies or further lab work may be required to rule out underlying issues in the brain or testes.

It’s essential to remember that only a qualified healthcare professional can accurately diagnose hypogonadism. Your doctor can then help you determine whether testosterone therapy is appropriate for your specific health goals.

Treatment Options

The good news is that male hypogonadism is treatable—and in many cases, symptoms can improve significantly with the right plan. The best treatment for your specific needs depends on the severity of your condition, the underlying cause, and your personal health goals.

Common treatment options may include:

Lifestyle changes: Exercise, weight loss, sleep optimization, and stress reduction can all improve natural testosterone levels, especially in men with borderline deficiencies.

Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT): For men with confirmed low testosterone and persistent symptoms, TRT can restore hormone levels to a healthy range. While it works differently for each patient, TRT has the potential to improve sexual function, bone density, mood, lean body mass, and more. (10) Options for TRT include injections, gels, or pellets prescribed and monitored by a physician.

Addressing underlying conditions: Managing chronic illnesses like diabetes, obesity, or pituitary issues can support better hormone regulation, improve overall health, and correct some cases of low testosterone.
