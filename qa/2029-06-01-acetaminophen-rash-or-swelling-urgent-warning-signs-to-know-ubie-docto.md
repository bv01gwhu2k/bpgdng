# Acetaminophen Rash or Swelling? Urgent Warning Signs to Know | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 7/21/2026

Can acetaminophen cause allergic reactions like rashes or swelling?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Acetaminophen can cause allergic reactions, though they are rare. Symptoms may include skin rashes, hives, itching, or swelling of the face, lips, or throat. In severe cases, reactions such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome or anaphylaxis can occur and require immediate medical attention.

Explanation

Acetaminophen, also known as paracetamol, is a common medication used to relieve pain and reduce fever. While it is generally considered safe, some individuals may experience allergic reactions to it. These reactions can manifest as skin rashes, swelling, or more severe symptoms.

Mild Reactions: Some people might experience mild allergic reactions, such as skin rashes or hives.

Severe Reactions: In rare cases, acetaminophen can cause severe reactions like anaphylaxis, which is a serious and potentially life-threatening condition. Symptoms of anaphylaxis can include swelling of the face or throat, difficulty breathing, and a rapid drop in blood pressure.

One study describes an anaphylactoid reaction, which is similar to anaphylaxis but does not involve the immune system in the same way. Another case report discusses acetaminophen-induced anaphylaxis. A third report highlights a case of anaphylactic shock caused by acetaminophen.
