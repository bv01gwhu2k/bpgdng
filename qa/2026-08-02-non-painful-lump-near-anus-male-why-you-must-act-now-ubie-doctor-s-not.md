What could a non-painful lump near the anus in males indicate, and should it be monitored?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

A painless lump near the anus in males is most often benign, commonly caused by conditions such as external hemorrhoids, skin tags, sebaceous cysts, or benign polyps. While these growths are typically harmless, any new lump should be monitored closely for changes in size, color, texture, or the development of pain, bleeding, or discharge. In rarer cases, a painless anal lump can signal a more serious condition, including anal warts, abscesses, or anal cancer, so persistent or growing lumps warrant medical evaluation.

Because symptoms can overlap between harmless and serious conditions, it's difficult to know the cause without a proper assessment. Explanation

Sometimes men can notice a small, hard lump near the anus that doesn't hurt. This lump could be a benign growth like an anal polyp or another harmless lesion, which means it is not cancer. In many cases, these lumps do not cause any problems and are found by chance. However, even if the lump is not painful, it should still be monitored. Watching for changes over time is important. You should look out for changes like the lump getting bigger, changing shape, starting to hurt, or causing any bleeding. While many benign lumps are safe and may not require immediate treatment, keeping an eye on it helps your healthcare provider catch any changes early.
