Why does inner knee pain occur when sleeping?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Inner knee pain at night is commonly caused by osteoarthritis, bursitis, or ligament strain, and it often feels worse during sleep. This happens because inflammation increases overnight, joint pressure builds up in stillness, and the body has fewer distractions from pain signals once you lie down.

Because inner knee pain can stem from many different conditions—ranging from minor overuse to more serious joint damage—it's important to identify the likely cause before deciding on next steps. Explanation

When you notice pain on the inside of your knee while sleeping, one possible reason is osteoarthritis, a condition where the joint wears down over time. Studies have found that as knee osteoarthritis gets more severe, people often experience more night pain. Inflammation in the joint can increase when you lie down, adding pressure on the knee, which affects sleep quality. When you are asleep, the lack of movement may cause stiffness and discomfort in the knee, especially if the joint's inner part (the medial side) is affected. Additionally, poor sleep quality itself can make the pain seem worse. Restless sleep or disturbed sleep can lead to a cycle where pain and lack of sleep make each other more pronounced. Keeping your legs comfortably supported with pillows may help, as it could relieve extra pressure on the knee during the night.
