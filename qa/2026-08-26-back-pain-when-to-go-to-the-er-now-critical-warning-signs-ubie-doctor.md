Go to the emergency room (ER) for back pain if it is severe and unrelenting, accompanied by numbness, tingling, or weakness in the legs, difficulty walking or standing, or loss of bowel or bladder control. These symptoms may signal a serious condition such as cauda equina syndrome, spinal infection, fracture, or nerve compression that requires immediate medical attention. Back pain following a fall, accident, or trauma, or paired with fever, unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer, also warrants urgent evaluation.

Not sure if your back pain is an emergency? Because back pain can stem from dozens of causes—ranging from muscle strain to nerve damage—guessing can delay critical care or lead to unnecessary ER visits. Explanation

Back pain can be caused by many things that span benign to life-threatening. Distinguishing between them can be challenging even for the seasoned clinician. Generally, extremely severe debilitating back pain, or pain accompanied by difficulty walking/standing, fevers/chills or loss of bowel/bladder control should prompt a visit to the emergency room. If you're unsure about the severity of your symptoms, you can check your symptoms with our free AI-powered tool to help determine if you have risk factors for more severe causes such as age (<18 or >50), immunocompromise, or underlying conditions such as cardiovascular, kidney, liver or bone disease.
