Internal Medicine

Vomiting is a forceful reflex where the stomach's contents are expelled through the mouth, driven by strong muscle contractions in the diaphragm and abdominal wall. While occasional vomiting is a normal protective response, repeated episodes can strain the digestive system and expose the esophagus, throat, and teeth to stomach acid. Over time, this acid exposure may cause enamel erosion, sore throat, acid reflux, and even tears in the esophageal lining (Mallory-Weiss syndrome). Dehydration and electrolyte imbalances are also common concerns.

Because vomiting can stem from many causes—ranging from mild stomach bugs to more serious underlying conditions—understanding your specific symptoms is the smartest first step.
