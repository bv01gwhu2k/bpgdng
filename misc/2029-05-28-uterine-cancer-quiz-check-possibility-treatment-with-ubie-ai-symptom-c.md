What is Uterine Cancer?

The vast majority of women develop post-menopausal bleeding as their first symptom of uterine cancer or cancer of the womb. Risk factors include age, with most cases appearing after menopause, a history of estrogen-only hormone treatment, and obesity. Diagnosis is after a pelvic exam, ultrasound, biopsy and other scans to determine whether the cancer has spread.

Diagnostic Questions for Uterine Cancer

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this disease:

Have you experienced any vaginal bleeding or unusual discharge outside of your period?

Do you have a lump in your abdomen?

Are your periods irregular?

Do you have stomach or abdominal pain with your abdomen becoming hard when gently pressed?

Are you experiencing pain or burning when you urinate?

Treatment of Uterine Cancer

After a gynecologist has confirmed the diagnosis through a biopsy, they will recommend a treatment plan. Uterine cancer is typically with a hysterectomy and other complex gynecologic procedures. Sometimes, additional treatments are recommended, including internal and external radiation therapy, hormonal therapy, chemotherapy, or a combination of these options.
