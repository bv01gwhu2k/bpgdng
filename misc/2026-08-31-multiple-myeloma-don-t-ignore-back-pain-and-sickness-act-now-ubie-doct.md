Multiple myeloma is a blood cancer that can cause back pain and a general feeling of sickness. It weakens bones—often in the spine—leading to pain, while also triggering fatigue, low mood, and even depression. Research links persistent back pain with declining mental health, which can worsen the sense of feeling unwell.

Because back pain and fatigue have many possible causes, from muscle strain to serious conditions like myeloma, it's important to understand your symptoms before worrying or waiting. Explanation

Multiple myeloma is a type of cancer that affects the bones and blood cells. It can cause back pain because it often involves the spine. The disease can make people feel sick in different ways.

Back Pain: People with multiple myeloma often feel back pain because the cancer can weaken bones, especially in the spine, making them hurt or even break.

Feeling Sick: The feeling of being sick can come from fatigue and depression, which are common in people with chronic back pain. Studies have shown that people with back pain often also feel very tired and can become depressed.

Mental Health: Feeling tired and sad can make it hard to do daily activities, and this can make someone feel even more sick. If you are feeling very sad or tired, you can visit 988lifeline.org for help.

In summary, multiple myeloma can make someone feel sick and have back pain due to its effects on bones and mental health.
