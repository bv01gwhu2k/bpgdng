Pain in the lower right abdomen combined with a numb leg is often linked to irritation or compression of the ilioinguinal nerve, which runs through the lower abdomen and into the upper thigh. When this nerve is affected, it can cause overlapping symptoms in both regions, including sharp pain, tingling, or numbness. Other possible causes include a hernia, muscle strain, or pinched spinal nerves, so an accurate diagnosis is important.

The ilioinguinal nerve runs through the lower abdomen and groin, and it can send signals down to the upper thigh and leg. If something, such as swelling, an infection, or a hernia, puts pressure on or irritates this nerve, you might feel pain in your lower right abdomen. At the same time, the same nerve or nearby nerves might also be affected, causing numbness or altered feelings in your leg.

Sometimes, a condition that causes inflammation or irritation in the nerve pathways around the lower abdomen can also lead to numbness in areas where the nerve travels. This type of spread of symptoms is known as referred pain, where the problem in one spot causes symptoms in another area served by the same nerve network.
