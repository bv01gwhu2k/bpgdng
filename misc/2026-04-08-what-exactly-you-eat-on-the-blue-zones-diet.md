# What, Exactly, You Eat on the Blue Zones Diet

If you tend to follow wellness trends, you’re probably familiar with the concept of Blue Zones. Those are areas where people live the longest and healthiest lives. These regions include Okinawa, Japan; Sardinia, Italy; Nicoya, Costa Rica; Ikaria, Greece, and Loma Linda, California. They’re places where people regularly live to celebrate their 100 birthdays, according to longevity expert Dan Buettner, author of the Blue Zones: 9 Lessons from People Who’ve Lived the Longest. Comparatively, in the U.S., the average person lives until around 78 years old.

Naturally, you may be wondering how diet plays a role in this concept and what you should eat to give you the best chance of longevity. Fortunately, a Blue Zones diet isn’t restrictive and is full of wholesome foods and ingredients.

What does the Blue Zones diet include?

One thing that likely won’t surprise most people is that the Blue Zones diet guidelines include a wide range of vegetables. (Note that the guidelines are principles for eating, not specific foods to eat at specific times of day.)

People who live in Blue Zones tend to eat the veggies that are in season and pickle or dry any excess to consume in the off-season. The most touted vegetables for longevity include leafy greens including spinach, kale, beet and turnip tops, chard, and collards. (Discover how to eat a day’s worth of leafy greens in 14 minutes.) These are complemented by other seasonal fruits and vegetables, as well as whole grains, all of which are eaten all year long. Consuming beans and nuts on a daily basis is a hallmark of Blue Zones diets as well.

The Blue Zone diet also encourages the consumption of plant-based oils over animal-based fats, as they offer a wide range of health benefits. Olive oil is the most frequently used one in the Blue Zone diet, as it’s shown to positively impact cholesterol levels.

While meat isn’t completely shunned in the Blue Zones, people who follow these guidelines consume it sparingly, usually on special occasions, in small side dishes, or as an accent in certain dishes. This applies to fish as well, with people in these regions consuming only up to three small servings a week. Additionally, dairy and eggs are limited in these guidelines. People in Blue Zones tend to eat eggs—often from chickens that roam and eat freely—just two to four times a week.

Finally, when it comes to beverages, the Blue Zones guidelines recommend mostly drinking water, coffee, and tea, as well as drinking red wine in moderation.

Blue Zones recipes

If coming up with a variety of recipes tends to be your downfall when it comes to healthy eating, fear not. The Blue Zones website offers an abundance of suggested recipes, which include a falafel salad, pastas with veggies, and sushi bowls, as well as desserts like strawberry-vanilla chia seed pudding and Dutch apple pie bars. Unsurprisingly, most of the available recipes are vegan or vegetarian, since minimal meat consumption is encouraged.
