# The Secret Weapon in the Closet of Professional Athletes

Either you've seen him or you’ve been him—the guy walking on a hot summer day, drenched in sweat, shirt completely soaked through.

Or maybe your neck and shoulder size are so different from your waist size that a shirt off the rack either chokes you to death or leaves you with enough extra fabric for another shirt.

[the Closet of Professional](https://pigmentlab.jcesqc8.com/blog/4614260389.html)

There's no dearth of problems ailing men since dress shirts were invented. Yet few advances have been made. You’d be hard-pressed to find anything but minute differences between dress shirts hanging in your closet and one your great-grandfather wore. And if you’re an athletic guy, you’re more likely to find the Holy Grail than to find a dress shirt that’s fits perfectly off the rack. These issues are things that that clothing companies, like Ministry of Supply, Vineyard Vines and even Under Armour, are finally starting to address.

To find out what it takes to make a shirt that is comfortable enough to work out in but still looks like a dress shirt, we reached out to one of the biggest names in the emerging space: Mizzen+Main. Today, they just launched a new collection with football giant JJ Watt, but more on that in a minute.

Before JJ, there was a man with a vision. Kevin Lavelle is the CEO and founder of Mizzen+Main. Lavelle was in college when he first had the idea of creating a better dress shirt. "It was in the summer, and I remember seeing this guy running into a building and he was just drenched," Lavelle told Men's Health, "And an hour later when he dried off, he looked even worse. Performance polos were just becoming to become mainstream, so I was thinking, 'Why can't you do that with dress shirts?'"

That was back in 2005, when Lavelle was young and didn't know how to advance his idea, but it stayed with him up until 2011 when he decided to give it a shot. By 2012, Mizzen+Main, named for sails on a boat in homage to the classic east coast lifestyle, had launched. Today, heaps of pro athletes and celebrities alike devour anything Mizzen+Main produces. Billionaire and Shark Tank investor Mark Cuban, who has no agreement or partnership with the company, constantly wears Lavelle's shirts on TV. Besides Houston's JJ Watt, Dallas Stars’ left wing Antoine Roussel and CrossFit legend Jason Khalipa have revealed that there isn’t much else they will wear.

Take Khalipa. He's a competitor, in more ways than one. He’s a household name in the CrossFit world, having competed in the CrossFit Games on eight occasions, taking home the title of “Fittest Man on Earth” in 2008. Khalipa is also a successful entrepreneur— he started his company, NC FIT, back in 2008 shortly after winning the Games. With the wide shoulders of a CrossFit champion, finding a shirt that fit his frame was nearly impossible. “I haven’t worn a shirt outside of Mizzen+Main for as long as I can remember,” Khalipa said, “When people see how my shirt fits, they always come up to me and ask about it, because it’s shocking to see a dress shirt fit a guy with my build so well.”

As a hockey player, Roussel has a different build. He doesn’t necessarily require the massive amount of wardrobe tailoring jacked guys like Khalipa and Watt require. But he has his own set of problems with the traditional dress shirt— for one, he lives and trains in Texas, and the last thing any guy wants to wear in the Texas sun is cotton. “Texas can get so hot, and these performance shirts breathe so well,” he said, “I don’t sweat nearly as much as in regular dress shirts. They almost feel like a shirt we train in.” Plus, Roussel's American pride swells when he dons a shirt. "All their shirts are designed and manufactured in the United States," he said. "People have a tough time finding jobs, and a company with a social conscience like that is really important to me."

JJ Watt knew he found his new go-to dress shirt the minute he tried it on. “My first thought was, 'How do I get involved with these guys?'" Watt told Men's Health. "It was truly the answer to all of my dress clothes woes."

"As a football player, we travel a lot. We leave the day before a game, wearing a suit, and wear the same suit home. Football players aren't the neatest guys; I'm not hanging up all of my clothes, so finding a shirt I can literally throw in my bag and not worry about wrinkles was huge," Watt said of the wrinkle-free fabric. "I love putting it on after a shower and knowing I'm not going to sweat through it or being comfortable on a plane ride home."

"I used to hate having to dress up. I'd dread putting on that uncomfortable suit and shirt," Watt admitted, "Now I'm the one suggesting going out, because I feel like dressing up." After a couple years, Watt and Lavelle started talking about how he could get involved in the company. "Kevin suggested that I design some shirts of my own. I thought that would be awesome."

Watt worked for six months designing his own line of Mizzen+Main shirts, a process he found fantastic but challenging. "There's so much more that goes into this than you could imagine. There's one shirt, for example, that's just a solid dark navy blue," Watt said. "Easy, right? Then you have to pick one blue from a book of 500 different shades of navy blue. Then buttons. There's a book of 1,000 of those."

Besides picking out every color, pattern and button for his line, Watt added a subtle personal signature to each of his shirts— a little white threading on the white cuff. But his favorite part, he said, came at the very end, when it was time to name each of the nine shirts. "We decided to name each shirt after street names from my history. So if someone comes up to me and says they're wearing one of my shirts, for example, the Park Hill, that's the street my grandpa grew up on. That personal touch means a lot to me."

A typical Mizzen+Main shirt goes for about $125, which Watt thought was more than reasonable. "I compare it to the price of a tailored shirt, and the cost to dry-clean that shirt. I think it actually ends up saving you money," he said, "It's the most comfortable dress shirt you'll ever wear."

Perhaps you already have a build like Watt, Roussel or Khalipa. If not, these guys are some of the fittest in the business, and they kindly gave us some of their best tips for hitting the gym.

The Assault Bike is your friend

For hockey, Roussel has to be fast. As one of the most aggressive guys in the sport, Roussel also has to stay strong and solid. "It's tough to find that balance between being fast and being solid," he noted. Besides doing "a lot of squats" and plyometric work, intervals on the assault bike are what he swears improves his fitness better than anything else. "I like doing 10 seconds on, 10 seconds off. I'll do eight reps of that, rest a bit, then repeat that two more times." Sometimes, he'll up those intervals to 20 or 30 seconds, on and off. "If I'm short on time and I only have 30 minutes before I have to go get on a plane or something, I'm doing the assault bike, every time," he said.

Master fundamentals to prevent injury

After a decade of competing, Khalipa only missed placing top 10 at the CrossFit Games once. In a sport that has a reputation for injury, Khalipa seems to have it figured out. "In CrossFit, people progress to more advanced movements before their body is ready, and that's where the injuries happen." he said. "People forget to master the fundamentals before moving on. Khalipa's advice? Master the three basics— the squat, the lift and the press— and incorporate those with some sort of interval CrossFit workout.

Always stretch and roll

Watt has undergone two back surgeries. The most valuable lesson Watt took away from the rehab was taking his time, with everything. "I spend so much time focused on building up my core now, building up my glutes, so it can support my whole body," he said. "I spend at least 45 minutes stretching and rolling out before and after each workout." While he acknowledges this may not fit into the schedules for most people, everyone should still make some time to take care of themselves. "Take stretching and rolling out really seriously," Watt advised, "Even if it's 10 minutes."

Skip the processed crap on your plate

Roussel avoids processed foods and eats organic whenever he can:"I also snack well; I eat a lot of nuts and fruits." Khalipa echoed that. "Eat real food from the earth and avoid sugar as much as you can," he advised. Watt's go-to snack, post-workout? "A peanut butter and jelly sandwich."
