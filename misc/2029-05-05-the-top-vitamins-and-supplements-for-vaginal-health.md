Taking vitamin E regularly may also help to keep the vagina lubricated and prevent dryness. Keeping vitamin E levels adequate may also help prevent and treat infections. Vitamin D helps to maintain normal cell function and prevent vaginal infections. Vitamin D has also been found 2 to improve vaginal pH and prevent dryness in postmenopausal women. Vitamin D supports the immune system, so deficiency may cause you to be more susceptible to infections that affect the vaginal and surrounding areas, such as yeast infections, UTIs, and bacterial vaginosis. Your body gets vitamin D through sunlight, but you can also take supplements to boost your levels. Natural sources of vitamin D include eggs, dairy products, and fish.

Vitamin C is commonly taken for its immune system support. It has antioxidant properties that help the body detox and fight infections. You can get probiotics by taking them as a supplement or eating certain foods high in probiotics. Food sources of probiotics include fermented foods such as sauerkraut, yogurt, and kombucha. You’re probably familiar with the mineral zinc. Many people take zinc to boost their immune system when they are feeling sick. In addition to supporting your immune system, zinc also supports proper metabolic function and wound healing.

Zinc is beneficial specifically for women in a variety of ways. One study found that a vaginal moisturizing gel containing zinc helped to improve vaginal symptoms such as pain, dryness, and itching in postmenopausal women.

Another study looked at zinc’s benefits for women with certain reproductive disorders, such as PCOS, painful periods, endometriosis, and vaginal dryness. This study found a variety of benefits of zinc for women’s health, some of which are listed below.

Zinc helps to improve tissue health and support healthy collagen levels, which may help to relieve vaginal dryness.

Healthy zinc levels can help to support proper hormonal levels, including female hormones such as follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH).

Zinc supplementation can help to improve symptoms of PCOS.

Taking zinc during your menstrual period can help reduce your period pain intensity.

Because zinc helps to support your immune system, it may help your body fight off certain infections, such as bacterial vaginosis or urinary tract infections.

Not everyone needs to supplement zinc; most people can get enough zinc from your diet through foods like chicken and red meat. However, if you have a zinc deficiency, you may need to supplement it. Talk with your doctor about whether zinc supplementation is good for you.

Magnesium serves a variety of important functions in the body. This essential nutrient ensures that blood sugar levels stay stable, supports muscle and nerve function, and supports bone health. Supplementing magnesium tends to be beneficial since many people are deficient in it. Healthy magnesium levels are especially beneficial for women since magnesium levels fluctuate throughout the menstrual cycle.

Magnesium supplementation may help with treating PMS and menstrual migraines. One study looked at transdermal magnesium usage as a treatment for vaginal conditions but was inconclusive.

Selenium has antioxidant properties, meaning it can protect cells from free radical damage, also referred to as oxidative stress. One of the many ways that selenium supports your health is by ensuring that the thyroid is functioning properly.

Your thyroid is an important gland in your body that supports hormonal health and affects a variety of functions in the body. Poor thyroid function has been associated with vaginal dryness, so making sure your thyroid is functioning properly by ensuring you’re getting enough selenium may help with some vaginal health issues.
