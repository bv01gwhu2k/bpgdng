Why PRP Treatments Sometimes Fail—and How to Maximize Success

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy harnesses your body's healing power to treat injuries, joint pain, hair loss and more. Yet not every PRP procedure delivers the desired outcome. Understanding why PRP results fail—and choosing the Best PRP centrifuge for clinics—can help you optimize treatment effectiveness. Below, we explore common failure points, elements of an ideal centrifuge, and key medical steps to boost your PRP success rate.

Why PRP Treatments May Not Work

Poor Patient Selection• Advanced osteoarthritis, severe tissue degeneration or systemic diseases (e.g., uncontrolled diabetes) may respond less predictably.• Unrealistic expectations—PRP often requires multiple sessions and several weeks to months for noticeable improvement.

Inadequate Platelet Concentration• Too low: Under-concentrated PRP may lack sufficient growth factors.• Too high: Excessively high platelet counts can inhibit healing by creating a "crowding" effect.• Optimal: 3–5× baseline platelet concentration is generally accepted in orthopedics and sports medicine.

Suboptimal Centrifugation Protocols• Incorrect spin speed or time can yield poor platelet recovery or excessive red-blood-cell (RBC) contamination.• Single-spin vs. double-spin methods: Double-spin protocols often achieve higher purity and concentration, but require the right device settings.

Activation Method Variability• Calcium chloride or thrombin activation can trigger immediate platelet degranulation; however, premature activation inside the tube reduces in-tissue efficacy.• Some clinicians prefer non-activated PRP, relying on in-situ collagen contact to induce release.

Injection Technique and Accuracy• Blind injections may miss the target structure.• Ultrasound guidance improves precision in joints, tendons and soft tissues—boosting clinical outcomes.

Patient Lifestyle and Compliance• Smoking, poor nutrition, NSAID overuse and lack of rehabilitation reduce PRP effectiveness.• Post-injection protocols (rest vs. activity) vary—proper guidance helps maintain an optimal healing environment.

Clinic Experience and Training• Inconsistent protocols among practitioners lead to variable results.• Dedicated training in PRP preparation, injection and aftercare is essential for reproducible outcomes.

Choosing the right centrifuge is foundational. Here's what to look for:

• Capacity & Tube Compatibility – Accommodates the tube volumes you need (e.g., 8 mL, 10 mL, 15 mL). – Compatibility with closed-system PRP kits reduces contamination risk.

• Ease of Use & Maintenance – Intuitive user interface with pre-set protocols. – Removable rotor heads for easy cleaning and sterilization.

• Clinical Validation – Peer-reviewed studies demonstrating consistent platelet yields, leukocyte counts and growth factor levels. – Manufacturer support for training, troubleshooting and quality assurance.

Troubleshooting Common Pitfalls

• Low Platelet Yield – Verify rotor calibration and centrifuge settings. – Ensure anticoagulant ratio and blood draw technique are correct.

• Excessive RBC Contamination – Adjust soft-spin speed/time to separate red cells more effectively. – Use a sterile pipette to avoid disturbing the buffy coat.

• Inconsistent Results Across Patients – Standardize protocols: tube type, spin parameters, activation method. – Train all staff in the same procedures and quality checks.
