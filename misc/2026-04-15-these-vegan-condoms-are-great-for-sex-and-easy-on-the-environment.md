To break down the confusing jargon of condom packaging, we enlisted the help of New-York based gynecologist Alyssa Dweck, MD FACOG.

Vegan

This means no animal products or byproducts of any kind were used. Vegan condoms are casein free. Plus, lambskins condoms or those made from sheep intestines are not vegan.

Natural

This label is pretty tricky, Dweck says, but in general this label means that the product is made with natural ingredients as opposed to synthetic or lab-created ingredients. Natural condoms don't benzocaine (a numbing agent used in lubes), nitrosamines (a potentially carcinogenic substance that's helps improve elasticity), parabens (chemicals used as preservatives that are endocrine disruptors), and synthetic fragrances.

Organic

"Organic typically connotes growth with no pesticides or synthetic fertilizers used," says Dweck. "This might apply to natural latex, which is plant derived and or any corn starch if present in ingredient list." Though take note: "organic” is not an accurate term to describe condoms since, condoms are not currently “certified” by any governing body. So if you do see a condom with an organic label, make sure to check the ingredients.

What to Look for in Vegan Condoms

When you're on shopping around for vegan condoms, look products with fewer (if any) chemicals, dyes, and additives. Be sure to avoid casein—a dairy-derived ingredient, commonly found in several brands. Vegan condoms don't test on animals and use non-dairy lubricants instead of casein to soften the latex. Also, some condoms on the market are made with lambskin, which doesn’t qualify as vegan.

What Are the Benefits of Vegan and Organic Condoms?

"In terms of efficacy, there is no difference between vegan and non-vegan condoms as both have to have about a 98% effectiveness against STIs and pregnancy," says Story.

But she says that condoms that are ethically and sustainable made can be easier on the planet. "Companies who follow ethical standards and use only non-animal by-products and don't test on animals are also the ones that are more environmentally conscious," she says.

Another great reason to go vegan: latex-free condoms don't have that rubbery smell that can be off-putting for some. "Less latex means they smell better," says sex writer Arnold.
