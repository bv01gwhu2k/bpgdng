Internal Medicine

Front-of-ankle pain is most often caused by anterior ankle impingement, a condition where soft tissue or bone spurs become pinched at the front of the ankle joint. It typically develops from repeated ankle movements (like squatting, kicking, or jumping) or lingering effects of previous sprains and injuries. Common symptoms include sharp pain when flexing the foot upward, swelling, and stiffness at the front of the ankle.

Because ankle pain can stem from many overlapping causes—tendonitis, arthritis, ligament damage, or impingement—identifying the right one matters for effective treatment. Explanation

Pain at the front of the ankle can be due to several reasons. One common cause is anterior ankle impingement, which happens when tissues at the front of the ankle are pinched during movement. This can occur in athletes or anyone who uses their ankle a lot.

Another reason for pain can be related to changes after an injury or surgery, like those involving the Achilles tendon. Such changes can lead to bone or tissue growths that cause impingement.

In some cases, pain might also be due to medial impingement, which affects the inner side of the ankle but can sometimes contribute to front ankle pain too.
