Why Your Doctor Checks Calprotectin While on Shilajit

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Shilajit is a natural supplement often used to boost energy and cognitive function, but its fulvic acid and mineral content may unpredictably alter gut flora or irritate the intestinal lining. Doctors use the fecal calprotectin test to detect subtle or overt intestinal inflammation and rule out IBD flare-ups or other gut conditions that could be triggered or worsened by supplementation.

Your baseline levels, dosage, and current symptoms all play a critical role in determining your next steps in care. Explanation

Shilajit is a natural supplement prized in traditional medicine for its potential to boost energy, support cognitive function, and promote overall well-being. Yet when you introduce any new compound into your routine—especially one that may affect digestion—your doctor may recommend monitoring gut inflammation markers, notably fecal calprotectin. Understanding why this matters can help you make informed choices and stay on top of your digestive health.

What Is Shilajit?

Shilajit is a sticky, tar-like substance found primarily in the Himalayas. It's rich in fulvic acid, minerals, and other bioactive compounds. Common uses include:

Enhancing mitochondrial function for more energy

Supporting brain health and memory

Aiding nutrient absorption in the gut

While many people tolerate Shilajit well, individual responses can vary—particularly when it comes to gut health.

The Role of Calprotectin as a Gut Inflammation Marker

Calprotectin is a protein released by white blood cells during inflammation in the intestines. Measuring its level in stool provides a noninvasive snapshot of how much inflammation is present. High fecal calprotectin can signal:

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) flare-ups (e.g., Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis)

Infectious colitis or other gut infections

Medication-related irritation of the gut lining

Clinical guidelines (e.g., from the American Gastroenterological Association) recognize fecal calprotectin as a reliable screening tool to distinguish between inflammatory disorders and functional issues like irritable bowel syndrome (IBS).

Why Monitor Gut Inflammation While on Shilajit?

Unpredictable Gut EffectsThough Shilajit is generally well tolerated, fulvic acid and other compounds can alter gut flora or interact with existing gut conditions.

Exclude Underlying IBDIf you start Shilajit and develop new or worsening digestive symptoms, checking calprotectin helps rule in or out active IBD.

Track Subtle ChangesLow-grade inflammation may not cause obvious symptoms but can be detected through calprotectin testing. Early detection allows timely adjustments.

Assess Supplement SafetyRegular monitoring ensures that Shilajit isn't inadvertently irritating your gut lining over time.

How the Calprotectin Test Works

The test is simple and noninvasive:

You collect a small stool sample at home using a kit.

Send the sample to a lab for analysis of calprotectin concentration.

Results usually arrive within a few days, reported in micrograms per gram (µg/g) of stool.

Typical ranges:

Under 50 µg/g: Normal (no significant inflammation)

50–200 µg/g: Mild inflammation—monitor and repeat if needed

Above 200 µg/g: Suggestive of active inflammation—further evaluation recommended

Your baseline (pre-Shilajit) calprotectin measurement

A mild uptick in calprotectin may warrant simply lowering the dose or pausing Shilajit. A significant rise could trigger more detailed testing, such as colonoscopy or imaging.

Tips for Safely Using Shilajit

Start with a low dose and gradually increase, watching for digestive changes.

Choose a high-quality, purified product free of heavy metals (look for third-party testing).

Keep a symptom diary to track any new or worsening gut issues.

Maintain communication with your healthcare provider—don't self-adjust medications without consulting them.

When to Reach Out to Your Doctor

Even if you feel fine, regular check-ins are valuable. Contact your healthcare provider if you experience:

Persistent or severe abdominal pain

Blood in stool or black, tarry stools

Unexplained weight loss

Prolonged diarrhea or constipation

If you notice any concerning digestive symptoms while taking Shilajit, check your symptoms using a free AI-powered tool to help you understand what might be happening and whether you need to see your doctor right away.

Balancing Benefits and Risks

Shilajit may offer genuine benefits, but it's not risk-free. Monitoring calprotectin ensures you're not overlooking emerging gut inflammation. By staying proactive, you can:

Maximize Shilajit's potential advantages

Minimize the chance of unrecognized gut damage

Address any red flags early, before they become serious

Final Thoughts

Your gut is central to overall health. Even natural supplements like Shilajit can have unexpected effects on your digestive tract. Monitoring calprotectin provides a clear, evidence-based way to ensure you stay on track—alerting both you and your doctor to any signs of inflammation.

Always remember: if you experience any life-threatening or serious symptoms, speak to a doctor or seek emergency care without delay. Regular check-ups, open communication, and smart use of monitoring tools can help you harness the benefits of Shilajit while keeping your gut health in check.
