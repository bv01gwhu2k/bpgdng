Chronic sinusitis: Often causes forehead pressure with nasal congestion

Common Causes of Temple Pain and Forehead Pressure

Several conditions can cause discomfort around the temples and the forehead. Identifying the root cause is key to effective treatment. Major possibilities include:

Chronic sinusitis

Tension-type headaches

Migraines

Temporal arteritis (giant cell arteritis)

Cluster headaches

Trigeminal neuralgia

TMJ (temporomandibular joint) disorders

Among these, temple pain and forehead pressure chronic sinusitis stands out when symptoms persist for weeks on end.

What Is Chronic Sinusitis?

Chronic sinusitis is an inflammation of the sinus lining lasting 12 weeks or longer, despite treatment attempts. Your sinuses are air-filled spaces in the skull around your eyes, nose, and forehead. When they become blocked and filled with fluid, germs can grow and cause infection.

Symptoms may wax and wane but never fully resolve

Often follows repeated acute sinus infections

Can be linked to allergies, nasal polyps, or structural nasal issues

When chronic sinusitis is the culprit behind temple pain and forehead pressure, you may notice:

Nasal congestion or stuffiness

Thick, discolored nasal drainage

Reduced sense of smell or taste

Facial pain or pressure (especially around the eyes, cheeks, or forehead)

Toothache or ear pain

A thorough evaluation helps distinguish sinus-related discomfort from other causes of head pain. Typical diagnostic steps include:

Medical History

Duration and pattern of pain or pressure

Associated symptoms (fever, nasal discharge, vision changes)

Past sinus infections, allergies, or nasal surgeries

Physical Examination

Checking for sinus tenderness by pressing around the cheeks, forehead, and temples

Nasal endoscopy: A thin, flexible camera inspects the nasal passages and sinus openings

Ear and throat exam to rule out associated infections

Imaging Studies

CT scan of the sinuses provides detailed views of sinus anatomy, blockages, or bone abnormalities

MRI may be used if soft-tissue evaluation is needed

Allergy Testing

Identifies environmental triggers that may perpetuate sinus inflammation

Laboratory Tests (if needed)

Blood tests to check for markers of inflammation (e.g., ESR, CRP)

In suspected temporal arteritis, additional blood work and sometimes a biopsy of the temporal artery

By combining these approaches, your doctor can pinpoint whether chronic sinusitis is to blame or if another condition is causing your temple pain and forehead pressure.

Other Potential Causes

Even with classic sinus symptoms, it's important to consider:

Tension-type headaches: Tightness or pressure wrapping around the head, often stress-related.

Migraines: Throbbing pain, typically one-sided, with nausea, light or sound sensitivity.

Temporal arteritis: Inflammation of arteries in the temples; can lead to vision loss if untreated.

Cluster headaches: Severe, one-sided pain around the eye, occurring in clusters over weeks.

TMJ disorders: Jaw joint issues causing referred pain to the temples and forehead.

Treatment Options for Chronic Sinusitis

If chronic sinusitis is confirmed, treatments aim to reduce inflammation, clear blockages, and prevent recurrence:

Saline nasal irrigation

Flushes mucus and allergens from nasal passages

Can be done with a neti pot or squeeze bottle

Topical nasal corticosteroids

Sprays (e.g., fluticasone, budesonide) to reduce swelling

Oral corticosteroids (short courses)

Used for severe inflammation, especially with nasal polyps

Antibiotics

For documented bacterial infections, typically after imaging confirms fluid-filled sinuses

Allergy management

Antihistamines or immunotherapy for allergy-driven sinusitis

Surgical options

Functional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery (FESS) to open blocked sinus passages

Polypectomy if nasal polyps are present

Your doctor will tailor the approach based on the severity of your symptoms, any anatomical issues, and your overall health.

Complement medical treatments with self-care strategies:

Stay well-hydrated to thin mucus

Use a humidifier to keep nasal passages moist

When to Seek Immediate Medical Attention

Although most sinus-related pains are not emergencies, seek urgent care if you experience:

Sudden, severe headache unlike anything before

High fever (above 102°F / 39°C)

Vision changes, eye swelling, or redness

Stiff neck or confusion

Weakness on one side of the body

Such signs may point to complications like orbital cellulitis, meningitis, or a neurological event. Always err on the side of caution.
